Salary Calculator Guide: Convert, Compare, and Understand Your Pay

📅 Published: July 4, 2026 ⏱️ 6 min read

Whether you are evaluating a job offer, negotiating a raise, or comparing salaries across locations, understanding exactly how much you earn — and keep — requires more than just knowing your annual number. This guide explains how to use salary calculators effectively.

Gross vs Net Salary: The Key Difference

Your gross salary is what your employer agrees to pay you before any deductions. Your net salary (take-home pay) is what lands in your bank account after:

  • Federal income tax (based on your tax bracket and filing status)
  • State income tax (if your state has it — Alaska, Florida, Nevada, and 4 others do not)
  • FICA taxes: Social Security (6.2%) and Medicare (1.45%)
  • Pre-tax deductions: 401(k) contributions, health insurance premiums, HSA contributions

Converting Hourly Rate to Annual Salary

If you are offered a $28/hour job, here is how to think about it annually:

  • Standard full-time: $28 × 40 hours × 52 weeks = $58,240/year
  • With 2 weeks unpaid time off: $28 × 40 × 50 = $56,000/year
  • With overtime (10 hrs/week at 1.5×): add ($28 × 1.5 × 10 × 52) = $21,840 extra

Comparing Two Job Offers

Never compare offers by gross salary alone. Consider:

  • Benefits value — health insurance, dental, and vision can be worth $5,000–$20,000+ per year
  • 401(k) match — a 4% employer match on a $60,000 salary = $2,400 free money per year
  • State taxes — a $75,000 salary in Texas (no income tax) is worth more than $75,000 in California (up to 13.3% state tax)
  • Cost of living — $80,000 in San Francisco buys less than $60,000 in Nashville
  • Paid time off — 10 days vs 20 days PTO is roughly a 2% salary difference on a $50,000 salary

Understanding Your Paycheck

Each paycheck shows deductions from your gross pay. Here is a typical breakdown for a $5,000/month gross salary:

  • Federal income tax: ~$625 (estimated, varies by bracket)
  • Social Security: $310 (6.2%)
  • Medicare: $72.50 (1.45%)
  • State income tax: varies by state
  • Health insurance premium: $150–400/month
  • 401(k) contribution (5%): $250

Negotiating Your Salary

Before negotiating, research market rates using Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) data, LinkedIn Salary, and Glassdoor for your specific role and location. Knowing the range gives you a confident, data-backed starting point.

Remember: it is always better to negotiate on the total compensation package, not just base salary. Flexible hours, remote work options, an extra week of PTO, or a signing bonus may be easier to negotiate than a higher salary in tight budget environments.
